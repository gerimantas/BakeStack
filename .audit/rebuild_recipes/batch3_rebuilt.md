## Tiramisu Cupcakes

**NOTE:** This recipe's title, intro, and Ingredients section begin BEFORE line 1450 (title at source line 1428, ingredients starting at 1436). It is included here in full because my range starts mid-recipe (at the FROSTING sub-section, line 1450). Even if traditional Italian tiramisu isn't your favorite, these cupcakes are sure to win you over!

Moist, espresso-soaked cupcakes paired with a delicate mascarpone cream create the perfect harmony of flavors that will capture your heart. 🤍

👌🏻 This simple recipe is always within reach! Enjoy, and don't forget to leave a ❤️ and save this post so you can easily find it later!

### Ingredients

INGREDIENTS (for 12 cupcakes):
· 150 g all-purpose flour
· 50 g almond flour
· ¼ tsp baking soda
· ¼ tsp baking powder
· ¼ tsp salt
· 70 g softened butter
· 200 g sugar
· 3 large eggs
· 150 g sour cream (15–20%)
· 18 g odorless oil
· 1 tsp vanilla extract
· 1 tbsp instant coffee

#### Frosting
· 500 g mascarpone
· 250 g heavy whipping cream (33–36%)
· 40 g powdered sugar
· ½ vanilla pod
· 1 tbsp Amaretto
· 1 tsp instant coffee, and cocoa for dusting
· A shot of espresso for soaking

### Instructions

1. Preheat your oven to 160°C (~320°F) and line a 12-count muffin pan with paper liners.

2. Combine the dry ingredients. In a separate bowl, cream the butter and sugar together, then gradually add room-temperature eggs.

3. Slowly incorporate the dry ingredients, followed by the sour cream, oil, and vanilla. Divide the batter in half.

4. Mix instant coffee into one half of the batter. Alternate spooning both batters into the cupcake molds, swirling with a toothpick for a marbled effect.

5. Bake for 20-23 minutes, then allow the cupcakes to cool.

6. To prepare the frosting, whip all the ingredients together until smooth. Drizzle each cupcake with 1-2 teaspoons of espresso, frost with the mascarpone cream, and finish with a dusting of cocoa.

Sift the stabilizer along with the powdered sugar you'll use to sweeten the heavy whipped cream.

🥣 Use approximately 7 g of stabilizer per liter of heavy whipped cream, or follow the package instructions. Add the sugar and stabilizer during the whipping process.

For gelatin, use the following proportions:

## Recipe for "Vanilla Pana-Cotta" Cheesecake

An incredibly tender dessert bursting with vanilla flavor while it melts in your mouth!

A crunchy base with crushed nuts and desiccated coconut combined with a delicate creamy cheesecake layer creates a unique taste that will be remembered for a long time.

### Ingredients

#### Crust
215 g basic crumb (recipe you can find in my stories, also will be pinned later in the highlights)
35 g desiccated coconut
30 g hazelnuts
30 g almonds
32 g coconut oil
15-25 g butter

#### Cream Cheese Layer
900 g cream cheese
250-300 g sugar (to taste)
50 g cornstarch
330 g whole eggs
650 g heavy cream (33-36%)
20 g vanilla paste
40 g lemon juice

#### Pana-Cotta
5.5 g gelatin + 33 g water
400 g heavy cream (30%)
20 g sugar
1 vanilla bean

### Instructions

#### Crust
📌 Slightly toast the nuts until golden brown, then let it cool and finely chop.
📌 Grind in pulses the crumb into fine crumbs in a food processor.
📌 Mix the crumbs with the nuts and desiccated coconut. Add softened butter and coconut oil and combine until homogeneous.
📌 Line the bottom of a cake form (mine is 20*30 cm) tightly with foil. Form the cheesecake base by pressing the shortcrust crumbs firmly onto the bottom. Place the cheesecake crust in a freezer for at least 30 minutes.

#### Cream Cheese Layer
📌 Beat cream cheese, cornstarch, and sugar in a mixer bowl with a paddle attachment until creamy.
📌 Gradually add eggs and mix well. Add heavy cream, vanilla paste, and lemon juice, and mix until smooth.
📌 Pour the cheese mixture into the prepared cheesecake crust and bake at 120-130°C for about 90-120 minutes — the finished cheesecake should jiggle slightly in the middle, like a pudding.
📌 Let it cool and refrigerate overnight for stabilization.

#### Pana-Cotta
📌 Soak gelatin in cold water and let it sit and bloom.
📌 In a saucepan, pour 130 g heavy cream with sugar and bring to boil. Add gelatin, stir until completely dissolved.
📌 Add the rest of heavy cream, vanilla seeds, mix, and pour over the cheesecake. Place in the fridge for 2 hours to set.

To remove the finished dessert from a cake form, warm it with a hairdryer from all sides. Cut the cheesecake into portions with a warm knife.

## Basic Savory Crumble Recipe for Cheesecake Crust

I vividly recall your persistent request to add savory flavors into our second online course on cheesecakes! Intrigued by the idea, I embarked on the delightful journey of creating impeccable savory cheesecakes.

A key challenge was to transform the sweet crumble into a savory counterpart. After a bit of experimentation, I successfully achieved the desired outcome, and I'm excited to generously share the recipe with you today 🎁

### Ingredients

BASIC SAVORY CRUMBLE

🔹 90 g butter 82%
🔹 20 g icing sugar
🔹 40 g whole eggs (~0.7 pcs)
🔹 150 g all-purpose flour
🔹 10 g cornstarch
🔹 40 g ground hazelnuts/almonds
🔹 3 g salt

### Instructions

📌 Combine softened butter and icing sugar until well mixed. Add the egg melange and mix again. Incorporate sifted flour and cornstarch, ground nuts, and salt. Mix everything with a mixer for 30–40 seconds until crumbly. Be cautious not to overmix the dough.

📌 Transfer the shortcrust crumbs onto a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, spread them evenly, and place in the freezer for at least 30 minutes. Bake in a preheated oven at 170–180 °C for approximately 15–20 minutes. Allow the crumble to cool completely. The baked shortcrust crumble should be crisp and golden.

The basic savory crumble is now ready!

📍to form the cheesecake crust with savory crumble, I typically incorporate some ground nuts, spices to taste, around 20–30 g of milk, 20 g of butter, and 15 g of coconut oil into the baked shortcrust crumble

## Authentic Namelaka Recipe from Valrhona School Research Kitchens

**NOTE:** Source line 1597 reads "UTHENTIC NAMELAKA RECIPE..." — clearly a raw-extraction dropped-letter artifact for "AUTHENTIC". Corrected in the title only per the "fix obvious extraction artifacts" instruction; body text left verbatim.

I know you've been eagerly awaiting the recipe, and here it is.

To prepare the namelaka, you will need:

### Ingredients

🔺 250 g dark chocolate 70%
🔺 5 g leaf gelatin (gelatin sheets)
🔺 200 g milk
🔺 10 g glucose syrup
🔺 400 g heavy cream 33–36%

🔌 an emulsion blender
🔪a heat-resistant silicone spatula, a bowl, and a saucepan

### Instructions

Method:

▪️ While stirring, melt the chocolate over a water bath or in short pulses in the microwave.

▪️ Soak the gelatin in cold water and allow it to swell.

▪️ In a saucepan, bring the milk to a boil, then add the glucose syrup and the squeezed gelatin.

▪️ Carefully pour 1/3 of the boiling mixture into the melted chocolate in a thin stream, stirring intensively with a spatula until a homogeneous and glossy mass forms.

▪️ Repeat the previous step two more times, incorporating the second portion of the boiling mixture into the chocolate, and then the remaining third portion.

▪️ Pour in the cold heavy cream and process the mixture with a blender until it's smooth.

▪️ Cover the namelaka with cling film, ensuring it touches the surface of the namelaka, and place it in the fridge to stabilize for at least 12 hours.

This is the original recipe.

Now, in advance of any questions you may have:

📌 Use chocolate and gelatin according to the percentage of cocoa. Instead of dark chocolate, for example, you can use 350 g of milk chocolate 40% + 5 g of gelatin or 340 g of white chocolate 34% + 4 g of gelatin.

📌 Leaf gelatin can be replaced with powdered gelatin by converting between different strengths of gelatin.

📌 Glucose syrup is added to give the namelaka a silky texture and extend its shelf life. If you prefer, you can replace it with honey or omit it altogether.

📌 If you don't have an emulsion blender, use a regular hand blender, making an effort to avoid incorporating any air bubbles, or mix the mixture until it's smooth using a silicone spatula.

## [UNTITLED: How to Make Perfect Chocolate Drips — technique/tips article, not a standalone dish recipe]

**NOTE:** Tagged `#marusya_theory_tips` in the source (distinct from `#marusya_recipe` used for actual dish recipes), confirming this is a technique tutorial, not a recipe for a baked good. Included as its own entry since it's clearly delimited and distinct from neighboring recipes, flagged as likely a different content-type than the recipe entries.

Chocolate drips are an elegant and seemingly simple decoration for a cake, in fact, they also require certain knowledge and skills.

Today we decided to reveal all the secrets, so grab your pens and notepads, and get ready to be all ears!

### Ingredients

#### Step 1. Dark Chocolate Drip Icing
You will need:

⚫️ 75 g dark chocolate 54.5%
⚫️ 25 g of odorless vegetable oil
🍴 heat-resistant silicone spatula
🍴 plastic bowl

#### Step 2. Applying the Drips
You will need:

🍴 pastry bag
🍴 scissors
🍴 offset spatula

### Instructions

#### Step 1. Dark Chocolate Drip Icing
While stirring, melt the chocolate over a water bath or in pulses in the microwave oven, add the vegetable oil and mix thoroughly until completely smooth.
▪️ melt the chocolate in short pulses, 5–8 seconds, stirring vigorously with a spatula after each pulse
▪️ do not overheat the chocolate, otherwise it will curdle – for dark chocolate, the maximum temperature is 50–55 °C
▪️before applying to the cake, the temperature of the icing should be 32–34 °C – if it is slightly higher or lower, you'll get either thick and rough drips (the icing isn't warm enough) or very thin translucent drips (overheated icing)

#### Step 2. Applying the Drips
Pour the chocolate icing into a piping bag and make a small cut. Pipe the slightly warm icing on the edge of the chilled cake, allowing some of the icing to roll off the edge and run down the side. Pour the rest of the icing into the center of the cake and spread it over with an offset spatula.
▪️to get neat drips, it's more convenient to use a pastry bag rather than a spoon – you'll be able to adjust the thickness of the drips by making a cut bigger
▪️ the cake should be well chilled as the cold surface slows down the drips, preventing them from running down the entire length of the cake
▪️apply drips at the same distance from each other, but make them of different length, adjusting it with the amount of icing piped out

## Recipe for Quiche with Salmon and Gorgonzola

The smell of baked salmon in a creamy egg sauce with pieces of gorgonzola and broccoli drives my family members crazy! They don't give the quiche a second to cool!

Check out the recipe, bookmark it and treat your loved ones to a new, savory pastry! I wonder if your quiche will have time to cool😏

#marusya_recipe

### Ingredients

#### Quiche Crust
▫️100 g high quality all-purpose flour
▫️70 g whole wheat flour
▫️100 g cold butter 82%
▫️18 g egg yolks (~1 pc.)
▫️30 g ice cold water
▫️1 g rock salt

#### Filling
▫️200 g broccoli
▫️250 g fresh salmon
▫️70 g Gorgonzola cheese

#### Sauce
▫️165 g whole eggs (~3 pcs)
▫️250 g sour cream 15%
▫️ salt and pepper to taste

### Instructions

#### Crust
✔️ Crust

🔸 Put the whole grain and all-purpose flour (both sifted) in the mixer bowl, add the cold diced butter and mix with a flex edge paddle attachment until crumbs form.
📎 if a flex edge paddle attachment is not available, you can chop the dough with a knife until fine crumbs form

🔸 Add egg yolks, water, salt and mix until the dough comes together. Immediately after this, stop the mixer. Slightly roll the dough out, wrap in cling film and refrigerate for 1–2 hours.
📎 if desired, you can make a bit bigger batch of the shortcrust pastry to seal cracks that may form at the joints of the dough during baking; if such cracks have formed in your crust, be sure to seal them to prevent the leakage of the sauce

🔸 Take the mold from the freezer and line it with a sheet of parchment paper, place pie weights on top. Bake in the oven for about 15–18 minutes at 180 °C, then remove the weights and bake the crust for another 10 minutes until golden brown.
📎 pie weights keep the crust from puffing up or shrinking away from the edge of the mold — you can use small ceramic or metal balls, as well as beans, lentils or peas

### Ingredients

#### Brownie

◾️15 g alkalized cocoa powder (22–24% cocoa butter)
◾️3 g baking powder
◾️1 g salt

#### Dark Chocolate Ganache
◾️180 g heavy cream 33–36%
◾️180 g dark chocolate 54.5%

### Instructions

#### Brownie
✔️ Brownie

#### Dark Chocolate Ganache
✔️ Dark chocolate ganache

📌 Pour the cream into a saucepan and bring almost to a boil while stirring, then pour it over the chocolate in callets and set aside for a few minutes. Mix until smooth.

## Recipe: Ganache for Cake Coating

As promised, I'm sharing with you a recipe for a basic ganache to coat the cake.

Today I'll reveal all the secrets of making the dark chocolate ganache🤎

### Ingredients

🟤400 g dark chocolate 54.5%
⚪️400 g heavy cream 33-36%

🍴 tall mixer or blender bowl, a thick-bottomed saucepan, a silicone spatula, a wide flat container, cling film
🔌 hand blender (optional)

### Instructions

Method:

▪️Place the chocolate in a tall mixer or blender bowl
📎I recommend using either chocolate callets or drops. If they are not available, break the chocolate bars into fine pieces.
▪️Add the cream into a saucepan and bring it almost to a boil. Pour over the chocolate and set aside for a couple of minutes.
▪️ Stir with a silicone spatula until completely homogeneous, trying to avoid incorporating air into the ganache.
📎 if necessary, process the ganache with a hand blender
▪️Transfer the finished ganache into a wide flat container, cover it with cling film touching the surface of the ganache and leave for incomplete stabilization in a cool place (ideally at 16-18 °C) for 3-5 hours.
📎 the wider the container you use is, the faster the ganache will stabilize

And a useful tip.

❔What consistency should ganache be for coating a cake?
✔️ When you tilt the container with the ganache, it shouldn't flow down, but at the same time, it should be very soft and pliable when you press it with your finger.

To celebrate the upcoming Easter, we'd like to share with you a recipe for panettone with incredibly juicy, soft and tender crumb!

So, grab your apron and enjoy working with an incredible panettone dough with a pleasant citrus aroma of candied tangerines!

#marusya_recipe

### Ingredients

#### Candied Tangerines
150–200 g tangerine peel
2 g salt
250–300 g caster sugar
1 vanilla pod
80 g rum

#### Rum Soaked Dried Fruits
60 g golden raisins
60 g dried cranberries
60 g candied tangerines
150 g rum

#### Bread Starter
250 g milk 3.2%
7 g caster sugar
7–8 g dry yeast
150 g strong flour

#### Panettone Dough
165 g whole eggs (~3 pcs)
150 g caster sugar
1 vanilla pod
lemon zest
zest of 2 oranges

400 g strong flour
2 g salt
100 g butter 82%
330 g rum soaked dried fruits

### Instructions

#### Candied Tangerines
✔️ Candied tangerines

Wash and pat dry the tangerines. Remove the peel, cut it into small pieces, place in a saucepan and pour water on top so that it completely covers the peel.
📌 Add 2 g of salt, bring to a boil and simmer for about 2–5 minutes. Drain the water. Repeat the same steps 2–3 more times to get rid of the bitterness of the albedo.

📌 Pour the warm milk into a small bowl, add sugar, dry yeast, mix until smooth and leave for 5–7 minutes until the mixture foams a little. Add the sifted flour, carefully combine with a whisk and leave in a warm place for ~ 30–60 minutes until the dough doubles in volume.
📍 if desired, you can replace dry yeast with 25 g of fresh yeast

## Recipe for the Perfect Cheesecake Crumble

As you probably already know, I am a perfectionist by nature, and the story of creation of this recipe is closely related to this trait of mine.

Here is how it was. Once, when tasting my new cheesecake, my family, who used to be treated to nice desserts, ate the cream cheese layer with great pleasure, but left the crust almost untouched.

An irresistible desire to make tasters enjoy both the cream cheese layer and the shortbread, inspired me to create an incredibly tasty, pleasantly crunchy shortbread crumble that disappears from the plate in a matter of minutes:

### Ingredients

🟠 95 g butter 82% (1)
🟠 80 g of icing sugar
🟠 18 g egg yolks (~1 pc.)
🟠 150 g all-purpose flour
🟠 10 g cornstarch
🟠 70 g ground hazelnuts/almonds
🟠 2 g rock salt
🟠 10-20 g butter 82% (2)
🟠 20-30 g coconut oil
🟠 10 g milk 3.2%

### Instructions

📌 Mix soft butter (1) and icing sugar until combined. Add the yolk, mix. Add flour, sifted with cornstarch, ground nuts and salt, and mix in a mixer for 30–40 seconds until crumbs have formed. It is important not to mix the dough for a long time.
📌 Transfer the crumbs to a baking sheet lined with parchment paper, distribute them evenly and place in the freezer for at least 30 minutes. Bake in an oven, preheated to 170–180 °C, for about 15–18 minutes, then cool down completely. The well-baked shortbread crumbs should be crispy and golden brown.
📌 Place the crumble in a food processor or blender and process it in pulses. Add 10–20 g soft butter (2), 20–30 g coconut oil and 10 g milk, mix thoroughly.
📌 Grease the walls of the cake ring with coconut oil or regular butter (the bottom of the ring should be tightly covered with foil in advance). Form the base of the cheesecake by pressing the shortbread crumbs first against the sides and then — the bottom of the cake ring. Then level the crust and make it smooth using a bent tablespoon. Put in the freezer for at least 30 min.
